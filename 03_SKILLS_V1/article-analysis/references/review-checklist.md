# Quality checklist for literature reviews (Track B)

Contents: 1 Question and protocol · 2 Search · 3 Selection · 4 Data extraction ·
5 Risk of bias · 6 Synthesis · 7 Heterogeneity and bias · 8 Conclusions ·
9 Currency and balance · 10 Transparency · 11 Subtype adjustments

The items follow the spirit of PRISMA 2020 reporting and AMSTAR 2-style
appraisal. Do not claim the review "meets" or "fails" a named standard unless
you checked each item against the PDF. Answer each item
**Pass / Concern / Fail / Cannot assess** with a page reference.

## 1. Question and protocol
- Is the question focused and answerable (clear population or topic, concept,
  outcomes, time frame)?
- Was a protocol registered or published before the review started?
- Are deviations from the protocol reported and justified?
- Is the scope matched to the title and conclusions?

## 2. Search
- Multiple relevant databases searched, with dates given?
- Full or reproducible search strings reported?
- Search terms include synonyms and controlled vocabulary where relevant?
- Grey literature, preprints, reference-list checking, or citation chasing
  used when appropriate?
- Language or date limits stated and justified?
- Was the search recent relative to publication date?

## 3. Selection
- Eligibility criteria stated before screening?
- Screening done by two or more reviewers, or checked by a second reviewer?
- Disagreements resolved by a stated method?
- Flow of records reported (found, duplicates, screened, excluded with reasons,
  included)? Do the numbers add up?
- Reasons for excluding full-text studies given?

## 4. Data extraction
- Extraction done independently or verified?
- Items extracted listed, including study design, sample, outcomes, and context?
- Handling of missing or unclear data in primary studies explained?

## 5. Risk of bias
- Was the quality or risk of bias of included studies assessed with a named tool?
- Was the tool appropriate to the study designs included?
- Were results of the assessment shown per study?
- Did the quality ratings change the synthesis or conclusions
  (sensitivity analysis, weighting, or exclusion), or were they only reported?

## 6. Synthesis
- Is the method of combining findings stated and suitable for the data?
- Narrative synthesis: are the grouping rules and weighing of studies explicit,
  or is it a list of findings?
- Vote counting used without regard to sample size or effect size?
- Meta-analysis: effect measure justified, model choice (fixed or random)
  justified, handling of dependent effects (multiple outcomes from one study)?
- Were primary-study comparisons actually comparable
  (similar populations, interventions, metrics, benchmarks)?
- For surveys: do the categories organize the field logically, or merely
  list papers?

## 7. Heterogeneity and bias
- Heterogeneity measured (I², tau², prediction interval) and explored
  (subgroups, meta-regression)?
- Publication or reporting bias assessed (funnel plot, tests, discussion of
  unpublished work)?
- Small-study effects or a few large studies driving the result?
- Sensitivity analysis, such as leaving out studies or changing assumptions?
- Overlap between included studies (especially in umbrella reviews)?

## 8. Conclusions
- Do conclusions follow from the included evidence and its quality?
- Is certainty of evidence rated (for example with GRADE or a similar approach)?
- Are weak, conflicting, or sparse findings stated as such?
- Do recommendations go beyond what the studies measured?
- Are research gaps identified from the evidence, not assumed?

## 9. Currency and balance
- Does the cited work cluster in one region, group, venue, or period?
- Are seminal or contradicting works absent (judge only from the reference
  list in the PDF, never from outside memory)?
- Is the review largely self-citation by the authors?
- For fast-moving fields: how old is the newest included study?

## 10. Transparency
- Funding and conflicts of interest declared, including relationships with
  the topic's industry or technology?
- Data, extraction sheets, and search outputs available?
- Limitations of the review process itself stated?

## 11. Subtype adjustments
- **Narrative review:** do not expect a registered protocol or flow diagram,
  but do check whether selection was transparent and balanced.
  Note the high risk of selective citation.
- **Scoping review:** risk-of-bias appraisal is often omitted by design;
  do not mark it as a Fail, but conclusions must not claim effectiveness.
- **Systematic review:** expect protocol, reproducible search, dual screening,
  and risk-of-bias assessment. Missing items here carry greater weight.
- **Meta-analysis:** all of the above plus Section 6 and 7 in full.
- **Umbrella review:** check overlap of primary studies and quality of the
  included reviews.
- **ML/CS survey:** check taxonomy quality, coverage of recent work,
  and whether benchmark comparisons use consistent settings.

## Severity hints
- **Critical:** conclusions contradict the included evidence; no stated
  search or selection method in a review that claims to be systematic;
  pooled results from clearly incomparable studies.
- **Major:** no risk-of-bias assessment in a systematic review, a narrow single-database
  search, unexplained heterogeneity, unrated certainty behind strong recommendations.
- **Minor:** missing registration for a narrative review, small count mismatches
  in the flow diagram, dated search by a few months.
