# Voice guide

Used in Step 4. It describes the voice and lists phrases to avoid. It is not a
source of content.

## The voice
A sharp graduate student who knows the field:
- Says the point first, then the condition.
- Uses the exact term, then plain words around it.
- Writes some short sentences next to some long ones.
- Qualifies only where the evidence needs it, with one hedge, not three.
- Does not announce importance; the content shows it.

## Ways to restructure a sentence (without changing meaning)
- Move the condition, cause, or result to the front or the back.
- Switch active and passive.
- Turn a noun phrase into a clause, or a clause into a noun phrase.
- Change the grammatical subject (study / result / variable).
- Replace a long list structure with a different grouping of the same items.
- Replace a generic verb with the specific verb the content supports.

## Avoid (English)
delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, pivotal, crucial, paramount, underscores,
highlights (as filler), showcase, foster, leverage, robust, comprehensive,
"it is important to note that", "it is worth noting", "plays a vital role",
"in today's world", "in recent years" (as an empty opener), "not only ... but
also" as a habit, "serves as a testament", closing sentences that repeat the
paragraph, three or more hedges on one claim, em-dash pileups.

## Avoid (Indonesian)
"perlu dicatat bahwa", "perlu diketahui bahwa", "sebagaimana diketahui bersama",
"di era modern ini", "di zaman sekarang", "seiring perkembangan zaman",
"memainkan peran penting", "sangat krusial", "sangat penting sekali",
"tidak hanya ... tetapi juga" as a habit, "menyelami", "mengungkap lanskap",
"ranah" as filler, stacked intensifiers ("sangat", "amat", "sekali") on one
claim, closing sentences that repeat the paragraph.

## Do not
- Add typos, slang, contractions, or personal opinion.
- Replace a precise term with a vaguer one.
- Make the text sound more certain or more sophisticated than the original.
